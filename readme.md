# Deep Learning + DOA Estimation: DA-MUSIC: Data-Driven Deeply Enhanced MUSIC DOA Estimation Algorithm [with code]

The primary contributions in source separation, localization, and tracking are key tasks in sensor array processing. Particularly, the estimation of Direction of Arrival (DoA) for multiple broadband and possibly coherent signal sources plays a crucial role in a wide range of applications, including radar, communication, image analysis, and speech enhancement [2]-[4]. Over the past few decades, numerous DoA estimation algorithms have been proposed, and this problem has been an active area of research [5]-[6].
The main schemes adopted in many DoA applications are the popular Multisignal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm, which provides an asymptotic unbiased estimate of the number of existing incident wavefronts, their approximated frequencies, and their DoA. Musical and other classical methods, such as conventional beamforming [8] and MVDR beamforming [9], are based on knowledge from underlying statistical models; this reliance leads to several key limitations. One disadvantage of model-based (MB) approaches is the inherent limitations of the signal models they rely upon, which consider narrowband signals. For example, this results in the inability to consistently estimate the DoA of correlated (coherent) signals, as well as the inability to resolve closely spaced signals with insufficient sample numbers or low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) [5]-[7].
To extend the narrowband model to broadband DoA estimation, various extensions and alternative methods have been explored because when the impinging signal is broadband, multiple frequency bands can carry different information about the angle of incidence at hand. Typically, these from narrowband to broadband extensions can be divided into coherent and non-coherent processing methods. In coherent processing methods, the covariance matrices of observations at different frequency points are combined using specific transformation matrices for coherence. Most coherent techniques are based on the concept of Coherent Signal Subspace (CSS), which concentrates the covariance transformations at different frequencies into an alternative narrowband model but uses different transformation matrices. Non-coherent broadband processing methods combine joint harvesting of DoA estimates obtained separately at each frequency point [12].
Recently, data-driven (DD) and deep learning (DL) have achieved success across a wide range of disciplines, leading to the development of DoA estimators with neural networks (NN) assistance [13]. Works [14]-[17] have implemented model-agnostic DoA estimation using dense NNs, convolutional NNs (CNNs), sparsely connected CNNs, and UNet architectures, respectively. Other methods [18] and [19] leverage knowledge from spatial covariance matrices, where [18] trains a CNN for classification tasks (which also allows it to use an unknown number of sources), and [19] analyzes pure DD estimates and combinations of DD methods with maximum likelihood estimation (MLE). Works [20] and [21] also combine DL with MLE using multiple dense NNs and ResNets to estimate subsets of candidate angles, respectively. [22] extends the architecture of [21] to estimate the number of sources using ResNet. Although such black-box NNs can handle array deficiencies due to their model-agnostic nature, they involve highly parameterized models that may be computationally intensive and lack interpretability similar to MB methods.
Or, NNs are used to robustly estimate the discrete MUSIC spectrum as a hybrid MB/DD system [23], [24]. Specifically, work [25] and [26] propose estimating the discrete MUSIC spectrum from the measured (spatial smoothed) covariance matrix by leveraging multiple convolutional NNs. Although these methods are more robust to model inaccuracies compared to the original MUSIC algorithm, using the MUSIC spectrum as labels for training results in the same drawbacks as the MB. The other DD method proposed in [27] considers systems with subarray sampling and uses NNs to obtain an individual estimation of the covariance matrix from non-coherent subarray measurements. This NN-assisted estimation is used for DoA recovery based on the subspace-based MUSIC algorithm. This method addresses the fundamental dependence of MUSIC on the estimated covariance matrix, thereby making the MUSIC algorithm more robust. However, it does not fully leverage the capabilities of NNs to improve MUSIC, as the NNs use the true covariance matrix as labels for training without considering its downstream task. Additionally, work [28] presents a hybrid MB/DD method that includes full row Toeplitz matrix reconstruction (FTMR) matrix [29] feature value decomposition (EVD) and a classification deep NN for estimating class MUSIC spectra and detecting the number of sources present via characteristic vectors and values.
Works [30], [31] apply CNNs to single-source broadband DoA estimation. However, these two methods use very different preprocessing methods and are trained as classification and regression tasks respectively. While the classification method restricts itself to a fixed resolution, the regressor in [31] directly outputs the DoA angles. Therefore, it can achieve arbitrary resolution but is limited to spatial covariance matrices as inputs. Another method [32] first decomposes broadband signals into overlapping narrowband components and uses support vector regression to obtain DoA.
The existing MB and DD DoA estimation algorithms' limitations in parsing multiple signals that may be broadband and coherent have motivated the derivation of an NN-assisted DoA estimator, which can leverage data to enable MUSIC to successfully operate in such scenarios.
We introduced Deep Augmented MUSIC (DA-MUSIC) in [1], a hybrid MB/DD implementation of MUSIC, leveraging the structure of classical subspace algorithms while enhancing them with NNs for learning to improve performance. The proposed architecture overcomes the fundamental limitations of MUSIC, enabling it to accurately detect the positions of coherent sources. Our design is based on the insight that model mismatch sensitivity and the inability of MUSIC to handle coherent and broadband sources stem from the noise and signal subspace estimation through the EVD of the empirical covariance matrix. Therefore, the proposed DA-MUSIC improves this critical step by obtaining an alternative pseudo covariance matrix from measurements via a recurrent NN (RNN), which learns along with serving as a peak finder. This work proposes a crucial extension of DA-MUSIC, enabling it to successfully estimate the number of non-coherent and coherent signals in unknown and varying numbers of signal sources. The additional neural enhancement allows for the classification of noise and signal feature vectors through training a classifier to learn. Numerical results show that MUSIC with 98% estimation accuracy outperforms 89% accuracy under similar non-coherent conditions. We further demonstrate the capabilities of DA-MUSIC in various SNR domains, three different broadband scenarios, and real seismic signals. DA-MUSIC not only concentrates frequency components but also simultaneously focuses on mutually dependent angles to receive stable bearing estimates.

Simulation results  
Conclusions propose an implementation of DoA estimation for a hybrid MB/DD MUSIC algorithm. The proposed DA-MUSIC is shown to alleviate some limitations and drawbacks of classical methods. DA-MUSIC can operate with unknown number of sources and wideband signals, and it is robust against severe mismatches in array geometries. The suggested hybrid MB/DD method provides a viable alternative, operating in both low and high SNR regimes, and shows significant resolution capabilities compared to MB and DD baselines across various settings.
